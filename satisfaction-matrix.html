Abstract

Occupational Satisfaction Matrix: a verification-first synthesis of job-satisfaction evidence across occupations, within disciplines, and at the fit meta-analytic layer. We assembled every load-bearing figure from the cross-occupation NORC/GSS ranking (Smith 2007), within-discipline studies in healthcare (Halasy 2021; Shanafelt 2011–2023; Medscape 2024; Merck 2020; Lo Sasso/Starkel 2015), law (Krieger & Sheldon 2015; After the JD III; Heinz, Hull & Harter 1999; Australian judicial surveys), and academia (French 2020; Bozeman & Gaughan 2011; Frei & Grund 2022), plus the interest-fit meta-analytic layer (Hoff 2020; Wiegand 2021; Michaelis & Findeisen 2022; Kristof-Brown 2005) — and subjected every figure to two adversarial verification rounds before display. Round one, run under a network policy that blocked nearly all primary sources, capped most figures at consistent-secondary (T2); round two, after egress opened, checked primary texts directly — promoting the full NORC occupation tables, the six-wave physician-burnout series, and four discipline studies to primary-verified (T1), recovering three previously unverifiable cells (one exposing a digit-transposition typo in the source report's own prose), and correcting five circulating claims. Three results organize the artifact. First, self-determination factors dominate well-being where credentials and pay do not (autonomy r=.66 vs income r=.192 and law review r=.00, N=6,226; judges > public service > large-firm prestige practice). Second, measured interest–work fit predicts satisfaction only weakly (ρ=.19, k=105, N≈39,602), with a primary-verified null for interest and skill congruence in German vocational training (N=4,097) — structure appears to matter more than content-matching. Third, verification itself is a finding: twelve circulating claims failed checking or required correction, and every cell without a verifiable figure is rendered as explicit NO DATA. A fourth, computed result then tests the interpretive layer itself: an original linkage analysis joining O*NET work-modality measurements to GSS occupation-level satisfaction (128 occupations, recomputed from microdata this session and validated against the primary NORC table to within 3.3 points) finds occupational prestige the strongest raw correlate (r=.57) but autonomy the only modality construct surviving prestige-and-education controls (partial r=.23, p=.0095); physicality and consequence-of-error wash out, and two axes (feedback speed, task identity) are unmeasurable — O*NET has no such elements. Confidence tiers, metric definitions, and sample sizes travel with every number; the six-axis work-modality framework is included strictly as an interpretive layer, visually segregated from measured data and scored against the linkage test.

    Methodology & limits

    Built 2026-07-07 by a multi-agent research pipeline: five parallel research agents (one per domain) extracted figures and citations; independent verifier agents then adversarially re-checked every figure with freshly phrased searches (seed numbers excluded from queries to avoid echo confirmation) and primary fetches, across two rounds.

    Round one ran under a network egress policy that returned HTTP 403 for almost all publishers and archives; only the Krieger & Sheldon (2015) PDF passed, making it the sole T1 cluster at first publish. Round two ran after the egress policy was opened mid-session: the NORC tables (GSS News No. 21), official PubMed abstracts (via NCBI E-utilities), the After the JD III and Indiana Law Journal PDFs, and the Springer full text of Frei & Grund were fetched directly — promoting most load-bearing figures to T1, recovering three NO DATA cells, adding two occupations, and producing five corrections (all disclosed in the “failed verification & corrections” list). Still unfetchable after round two: ScienceDirect (Hoff, Wiegand, French), Wiley (Kristof-Brown), Medscape (registration wall), and Merck — those figures remain T2, and the middle Medscape specialties remain NO DATA.

    No figure was simulated, interpolated, or estimated. Conflicting values are displayed side by side. Different metrics (% very satisfied, burnout %, well-being composites, correlation coefficients) are never merged onto a shared scale. Sample sizes are shown where verified and marked unknown where not.

    The linkage panel is an original computed analysis, not a citation: GSS cumulative microdata (downloaded from gss.norc.org) recomputed to occupation-level satisfaction, joined to O*NET 30.3 modality measurements through the NIOSH census-code crosswalk chain, validated against the primary NORC table (mean absolute delta 3.3 points across 23 matchable occupations). The reproducible pipeline (four Python scripts), the full method log, per-occupation crosswalk-quality flags, and sensitivity runs (n≥50; 1988–2010; unweighted) ship in analysis/linkage/ in the same repository.
