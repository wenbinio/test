Smith, T.W. (2007), Job Satisfaction in the United States, NORC/University of Chicago, pooled General Social Survey data (>27,000 respondents). The published report ranked occupations at the top and bottom of the distribution; the middle was not retrievable this session.

Ranking trap, shown not hidden: several secondary write-ups call roofers the single least-satisfied occupation. In the verified figures, laborers-except-construction (21.4%) sit lower on satisfaction; roofers are lowest on the report's separate general happiness measure (14% “very happy”), the likely source of the confusion. Lawyers, physicians, and university faculty do not appear in the retrievable top/bottom lists — their discipline panels below use discipline-specific studies with different metrics, kept in separate panels by design.

Faculty time-allocation profiles — French et al. 2020 T2

metric: satisfaction ordering across profiles (no retrievable means)

Seed correction applied: a claim circulated that the teaching-heavy profile also had the highest turnover intention. The paper found no significant profile differences in turnover intentions or affective commitment — only satisfaction and work-family balance differed. The wrong claim is listed in the “failed verification” panel.

    Methodology & limits

    Built 2026-07-07 by a multi-agent research pipeline: five parallel research agents (one per domain) extracted figures and citations; an independent verifier agent then adversarially re-checked every figure with freshly phrased searches (seed numbers excluded from queries to avoid echo confirmation) and attempted primary fetches.

    Session constraint: this research environment's network egress policy blocked direct fetches to almost all publishers and archives (HTTP 403 at the gateway). Web search worked. Exactly one primary source passed the gateway — the Krieger & Sheldon (2015) PDF — which is why that study is the only T1 cluster. All other figures are capped at T2 (multi-query-consistent secondary evidence) or T3 (contested). A follow-up session with open egress could promote T2 figures to T1 against primary texts.

    No figure was simulated, interpolated, or estimated. Conflicting values are displayed side by side. Different metrics (% very satisfied, burnout %, well-being composites, correlation coefficients) are never merged onto a shared scale. Sample sizes are shown where verified and marked unknown where not.
