A

What the three answers mean

The walkthrough sorts you into one of three groups. They are the same three the warning signs page uses, and the logic is deliberately cautious: where it is uncertain, it tells you to be seen.

  • Go now Something you ticked in the emergency screen. Do not wait to see whether it settles, do not wait for a GP appointment, and do not drive yourself if you feel faint or are bleeding heavily.
  • Book within days Visible blood, symptoms lasting three weeks or more, or one of the other patterns that changes how quickly you should be assessed. You do not need to know what it is. Ask for the earliest appointment and name the symptom.
  • Probably nothing, but keep the note Short-lived symptoms with no blood and no other flags. Most of these settle. Keep logging, and come back to this page if anything on the first two lists appears.

The note builder cannot diagnose you and does not try to. It sorts by urgency and writes down what you said, which is a genuinely useful thing and a strictly limited one.

B

Where your answers go

Nowhere. This page has no server, no analytics and no network requests of any kind — open your browser's network tab and watch it stay empty. Your answers are held in your browser's own local storage so that closing the tab does not lose the log, and the Clear everything button deletes them.

If you are in private browsing, your browser will refuse to store anything at all. The page detects that, tells you at the top, and keeps working for the current session only.

This is information, not medical advice

OUTPUT cannot examine you, does not know your history and is no substitute for a clinician who can. Anything described here as urgent should be treated as urgent. If something feels badly wrong to you and this site does not mention it, that is a reason to be seen, not a reason to wait. Nothing here is treatment advice and no page gives drug doses.

Figures are drawn from mainstream clinical sources and given as ranges. In the UK, NHS 111 will triage by phone or online at any hour; elsewhere, use your local urgent-care line. Nobody is annoyed with you for turning up and being fine.
