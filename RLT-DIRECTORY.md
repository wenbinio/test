# Singapore RLT — where to send an application

Relevant Legal Training: the six months an overseas Scheduled University graduate serves before becoming
a **qualified person**. RLT and the practice training contract are **different applications** — many firms
run two separate listings.

`RLT (6 mths) → Part A → Part B → PTP (12 mths) → admission`

Interactive version: `rlt-directory.html`. Checked **31 July 2026**; live cycle is RLT 2027 / PTC 2028.
**87 practices, 110 verified addresses.**

---

## Check these two before you send anything

- **You must be a Singapore citizen or permanent resident.** Rule 8(1)(a) of the Legal Profession
  (Qualified Persons) Rules makes this a condition of the overseas route, alongside the degree and the six
  months. No amount of RLT substitutes for it.
- **Check which Schedule your university sits on.** The Second Schedule limb now requires the degree to
  have been conferred **before 1 January 2025**, closing that route for later graduates. The First
  Schedule list is unaffected.

## Rules that decide whether the six months counts

- **6 months inside a continuous 8-month window**, full or half days only. Serve over eight months and the excess is not banked.
- **Weekends and public holidays count** toward the period but cannot be your start or end date — except that where you owe make-up days, the calculated last day may fall on one immediately before the make-up period.
- **No free leave allowance.** Every non-working day extends the end date. The practice training period allows 18 days; RLT allows none. Three hours off costs **half a day**.
- **Firm closure days earn nothing**, even if you worked through them.
- **Finish before Part B begins**, after all make-up. Build a buffer.
- **RLT need not be served in Singapore** — unlike the practice training period, there is no residence requirement, and SILE's own worked examples use UK firms on the UK calendar.
- **Paralegal or internship work does not count** on its own — but certification can be given **retrospectively** if the practice will certify a formal supervised training arrangement. Agreeing it up front is far safer.
- **Nine certification templates**, one per category. **None covers the UK pupillage limb** — contact SILE if that is your route.
- **You can stitch periods together** across organisations within the eight-month window.
- **The moratorium does not apply to RLT at all** — it governs practice training contracts only. There is no standing "1 July rule"; that date is simply what the formula yields for one cohort.
- **The public-sector supervisor route covers seven bodies:** IRAS, IPOS, SLA, MPA, NEA, PUB, Public Defender's Office. **MAS, CPF, LTA, HDB, JTC, EDB and IMDA are not on the list.**
- **The Legal Service Officer route is circular** — the LSC requires you to already be a qualified person, so it sits downstream of RLT.
- **The in-house limit governs *relevant legal work*, a separate limb — not RLT.** Where it applies: the **equity** securities of the employing entity itself must be listed. Debt-only listings fail; a private subsidiary of a listed parent fails. No waiver exists.
- **You may not need RLT at all.** Relevant legal *practice or work* is an alternative limb: active practice as a lawyer outside Singapore or as a foreign lawyer here. If you are already qualified elsewhere, often cleaner.
- **Pay: budget $1,000–3,000/month for RLT.** No mandated minimum. The quoted S$40–55k annual band is for *practice trainees*, post-Part B. Angeline Suparto pays $2,000 RLT vs $2,800 PTC at the same desk.
- **Do not sign with two practices** to keep options open — Guidance Note 3.9.1 says it is neither advisable nor appropriate.
