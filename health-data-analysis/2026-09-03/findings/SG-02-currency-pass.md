### A. HIA 2026 s 25 — what it actually provides

**Read from the gazetted Act:** `https://assets.egazette.gov.sg/2026/Legislative%20Supplements/Acts%20Supplement/02.pdf` (Acts Supplement No. 2 of 2026, 157 pp) — **Retrieved successfully: yes** (1,287,877 bytes; text extracted with PyMuPDF).

**s 25 is not a free-standing provision.** It sits in **Part 2 (National Electronic Records System), Division 4 — Derived information**, which comprises **ss 24–28**:

| s | Heading (from the Act's own arrangement of sections and body headings) |
|---|---|
| 24 | Interpretation of this Division |
| **25** | **Applications for derived information** |
| 26 | Access, collection, disclosure and use of derived information |
| 27 | Obligations of requestors |
| 28 | Directions relating to requestors |

**The operative words of s 25(1)–(3)**, quoted from the Act:

> "A person who satisfies the prescribed criteria and requirements or a public agency may apply to the Minister to obtain derived information in accordance with this section." — s 25(1)

> "The Minister may approve an application to obtain type 1 derived information if the application is made for a purpose that relates to or promotes public health." — s 25(2)

> "The Minister may approve an application to obtain type 2 derived information if the Minister is satisfied … that it is in the public interest to do so." — s 25(3)

**The surrounding definitions (s 24 and s 6), quoted:**

> "'type 1 derived information' means derived information that is in the form of individually-identifiable health information" — s 24

> "'type 2 derived information' means derived information that is in the form of aggregated or anonymised health information" — s 24

> "'section 25 approval' means an approval granted under section 25(6)(a)" — s 24

> "'derived information' means health information that is derived from accessible health information about one or more individuals" — s 6 (the definition continues: "and includes an extract or a copy of any such accessible health information")

> "'requestor' means a person who is approved to obtain derived information under section 25(2) or (3)" — s 6

**The rest of the s 25 machinery, in outline (all from the Act's own text):**

- **s 25(4)** — form, manner and time "specified by the Minister"; the application must specify every purpose and whether the applicant intends to **onward-disclose**; and must be accompanied by "any information that may be prescribed" and anything else the Minister requires.
- **s 25(5)** — if onward disclosure is intended, the applicant must additionally specify the identity or description of the recipient and every purpose of that disclosure.
- **s 25(6)** — the Minister may approve, "subject to any conditions or restrictions in relation to the access, collection, disclosure and use of the derived information that the Minister thinks fit", or reject.
- **s 25(7)** — the Minister may substitute **type 2** for **type 1** where the stated purpose can be met by aggregated/anonymised information instead.
- **s 25(8)** — mandatory relevant considerations for a **type 1** application: public interest; whether it is "feasible and reasonable" to obtain the individuals' prior consent; and whether type 2 would do instead. **s 25(9)** — the list is not exhaustive.
- **s 25(10)** — an approval must specify the purpose; the named individuals or class within the requestor allowed to access; any permitted onward recipients and purposes; and whether the requestor may retain the information after the purpose ends.
- **s 25(11)** — the Minister may direct the System Operator to provide the information. **s 25(12)** — the Minister may revoke an approval by written notice.
- **s 25(13)** — breach of a condition on a s 25 approval is an offence: fine up to **$50,000** or imprisonment up to **2 years** or both, plus $1,000/day continuing.
- **s 26** — a requestor may access, collect, disclose and use derived information for an approved purpose **"without the prior consent of any individual to whom the derived information relates"**.
- **s 27** — requestor obligations: policies and practices; no disclosure or use of type 1 information (or of aggregated/anonymised information derived from it) outside the approval without prior written Ministerial approval; and a **delete-or-destroy** duty once the purpose ends.

**Key structural points for the user's reference:**
1. **There is no fee provision in s 25.** No fee is mentioned anywhere in ss 24–28.
2. **No timeline is fixed.** Time is "specified by the Minister" (s 25(4)(a)); no decision deadline is imposed on the Minister.
3. **The approval body is the Minister**, not an institutional review board, not a research access committee, and not the System Operator. The System Operator only supplies the data when directed (s 25(11)).
4. **Commercial applicants are not excluded, and not mentioned.** The gate is "a person who satisfies the prescribed criteria and requirements or a public agency" (s 25(1)). Everything turns on criteria that **have not yet been prescribed**.
5. **The regulation hook.** s 112(2)(e) empowers the Minister to make regulations for "the form and manner in which an application to obtain derived information may be made", and s 112(2)(f) for "any matter that is required or permitted to be prescribed under this Act" — which is where the s 25(1) "prescribed criteria and requirements" and the s 25(4)(c)(i) prescribed accompanying information must come from. **No such regulations exist.**
6. Consequence: **s 25 cannot presently be used by a non-public-agency applicant at all**, both because the Act is uncommenced and because the criteria a private applicant must satisfy are unprescribed.

#### B1. Derived information and applications for access — the finding is a **verified negative**

**The Implementation Guide v2.0 says nothing whatever about derived information, s 25 applications, secondary use, or research access.** It is a compliance manual for contributing to and reading from the NEHR and for cyber/data security. It does not describe:

- the meaning of "derived information", or type 1 versus type 2;
- who may apply, or on what criteria;
- the application route, approval body, criteria, timelines or fees;
- whether commercial applicants may apply;
- TRUST, or any other research data platform.

**How that negative was established.** The full 74-page text was extracted with PyMuPDF and searched. Counts over the whole document:

| Term searched (case-insensitive, whole document) | Occurrences | What they are |
|---|---|---|
| `derived` | 4 | 1 × password hygiene ("passwords are not derived from…", p 33); 3 × the Annex C sample confidentiality clause "any analysis, compilation, note, study, memoranda or other document derived from…" |
| `TRUST` | 6 | all the ordinary adjective — "trusted sources", "trusted networks", "trusted staff members", "trusted with your organisation's systems". **Zero references to the TRUST platform.** |
| `secondary` | 2 | both "Secondary backup" in the Annex D backup worksheet |
| `research` | 1 | Annex F sample policy, cl 5.5 (quoted below) |
| `Minister` | 0 | — |
| `anonymis` / `aggregat` / `de-identif` | 0 / 0 / 0 | — |
| `policy analysis` | 0 | — |
| `public health` | 1 | the Chapter 1 framework description, not an access route |
| `fee` | 2 | neither about applications |

**The single sentence in the whole Guide that touches secondary use** is in the model institutional policy at **Annex F, p 60, cl 5.5**:

> "Authorised individuals must not access the NEHR for administrative, audit, purely educational, or research purposes unless specifically approved by MOH."

That is a prohibition addressed to a provider's own staff, with an undefined "approved by MOH" escape. It is **not** the s 25 route, it names no application process, and it cites no provision. The adjacent cl 5.4 is the only other access carve-out:

> "…except if the access is for purposes of conducting a medical examination allowed for under the Third Schedule of the Health Information Act."

**The only HIA provisions the Guide pinpoints anywhere** are: **s 3** and **s 46** (in the Annex C sample confidentiality clauses, defining "health information" and "relevant information" respectively), the **Third Schedule** (Annex F cl 5.4), the **First Schedule** (contribution obligations, by cross-reference to the separate NEHR Contribution Requirements deck) and the **Second Schedule** (access eligibility). **Sections 24–28 are never cited.**

> **Bottom line for the user:** the operative guidance layer is silent on the one route in the Act by which health information leaves the care setting for research, analytics or commercial use. A private applicant seeking derived information has, as at 3 September 2026, **no published criteria, no form, no fee schedule, no timeline and no named decision-maker below the Minister.**

### ⚠️ Method correction the project should adopt

The query body supplied in my task brief —

```json
{"query":"Health Information Act","hitsPerPage":50,"filters":"publishTimestamp >= 1755216000000"}
```

— **runs without error but the date filter is silently ignored.** `publishTimestamp` is indexed as a **string** (`"1785834000000"`), not a number, so the numeric comparison never applies. That query returned 465 hits including records from 2025. Anyone who used it believing it was date-restricted was reading an unfiltered relevance ranking.

**The reliable date restriction is the `publishDate` facet** (values are `DD/MM/YYYY` strings and *are* faceted; `publishYear` and `publishMonth` are too; `publishDay` is **not** a facet). The index also applies `distinct` on `objectGroup`, so one hit is returned per document rather than per text chunk, and Algolia's `paginationLimitedTo` caps any single query at 1,000 records — which is why the sweep below is done **day by day**.
