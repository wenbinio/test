A speculative RLT email that works

For the firms with no advertised round. Keep it under 200 words — partners read these on a phone. Replace the highlighted parts, attach one PDF, and send it to the practice manager or the named inbox. 

Subject: Relevant Legal Training 2027 — Your Name, University LLB

Dear Sir/Madam or named partner,

I am a final-year LLB student at University, graduating in June 2027. As an
overseas Scheduled University graduate I need to complete six months of
Relevant Legal Training before I can become a qualified person, and I am
writing to ask whether Firm would consider hosting me from August 2027.

I am drawn to your disputes / corporate / shipping practice because one specific,
verifiable reason — a reported case, a deal, a practitioner's writing. One line
on relevant experience: an internship, a mooting result, a language, prior work.

I am on course for a 2:1 / first. My CV, transcripts and a short writing sample
are attached as a single PDF. I am available for interview at any time, including
outside Singapore hours, and can be flexible on the start date within the
August–October window.

Thank you for considering this.

Your name
Phone · Email

Three things make the difference: name the exact months you are free, say RLT explicitly so nobody reads it as a training contract application, and give one concrete reason for that firm. Generic rounds get deleted; a specific six-month ask with a clear start date frequently gets a reply.

Rules that decide whether your six months counts

Verified against the Legal Profession (Qualified Persons) Rules, SILE's RLT FAQs and the Practice Training Period Guidelines. Get these wrong and the period is disregarded. SILE is the only authority.

Check these two before you send anything

You must be a Singapore citizen or permanent resident. Rule 8(1)(a) of the Qualified Persons Rules makes this a condition of the overseas-graduate route, alongside the degree and the six months of training. No amount of RLT substitutes for it. If you are not a citizen or PR, resolve that question first — it governs whether this pathway is open to you at all.

Check which Schedule your university sits on. The Second Schedule limb now requires the degree to have been conferred before 1 January 2025, which closes that route for later graduates. The First Schedule list is unaffected. Confirm your own university's position in the Rules before planning around it.

  • 6 months inside a continuous 8-month window, counted in full or half days only. Serve more than eight months and the excess is not banked — you simply declare an eight-month period.
  • Weekends and public holidays count toward the period but cannot be your start or end date. One exception: where you owe make-up days, the calculated last day may fall on a weekend or holiday immediately before the make-up period — it is the official end date, inclusive of make-up, that must be a working day.
  • There is no free leave allowance. Every non-working day is added to the end. This differs from the practice training period, which allows 18 days of absence without make-up. Three hours off costs you half a day — that is the minimum granularity.
  • Firm closure days earn you nothing, even if you worked through them. They must be made up regardless.
  • Finish before Part B begins — by the last working day before the course starts, after all make-up. Build in a buffer rather than finishing to the day.
  • RLT need not be served in Singapore. Unlike the practice training period, there is no Singapore-residence requirement, and SILE's own worked illustrations use UK firms with the calculation run on the UK calendar. A genuine asymmetry, and a useful one.
  • Paralegal or internship work does not count on its own. But the certification can be given retrospectively: if the practice will certify in writing that you were under a formal training arrangement receiving supervised training, it is recognised. It need not have been labelled a training contract at the outset — though getting it agreed up front is far safer than hoping.
  • No SILE registration beforehand — but at the end you submit a certification letter on SILE's template. There are nine templates, one per category; pick the right one. There is no template for the UK pupillage limb, so contact SILE directly if that is your route rather than forcing the foreign-law-practice form.
  • You can stitch periods together across organisations, so long as the total hits six months inside the eight-month window. Two three-month seats at small firms is a valid plan B.
  • The moratorium does not apply to RLT at all. It governs practice training contracts only. And there is no standing "1 July rule" — 1 July 2026 is simply what the formula yields for one cohort. The rule: if you are not yet a qualified or eligible person, you may apply for a PTC no earlier than one year before the first day of the month of the earliest Part B session you could take in ordinary progression. It binds only those eligible for Part B in July 2027 or later. Exempt: qualified and eligible persons (any time), anyone eligible for Part B before July 2027, those who secured a contract before 1 October 2024 for a January 2028 start, and anyone whose progression was disrupted.
  • The in-house restriction is narrower than it sounds — and it is not a limit on RLT. The securities-exchange test governs relevant legal work, a separate limb. Where it applies, the rule is strict: the equity securities of the employing entity itself must be listed, so a debt-only listing fails, and a private subsidiary of a listed parent fails. No waiver or discretion exists. Failing it does not block you from the RLT route through a law practice.
  • Pay is unregulated, and RLT pays below trainee rates. There is no mandated minimum — the Minister for Law has said imposing one would be prescriptive and could reduce training places at smaller firms. Guidance Note 3.9.1 asks only that a practice cover basic transport and meal costs. Budget against $1,000–3,000/month for RLT specifically. Only two firms publish an explicit RLT figure: Lee & Lee at $3,000 and HOH Law at $1,000–2,500. The widely quoted S$40–55k annual band is for practice trainees, who sit post-Part B — do not plan around it for your six months. The register bears this out: Angeline Suparto pays $2,000 for RLT against $2,800 for a practice trainee at the same desk. The term of art is honorarium; the Guidance Note asks practices to avoid "allowance" or "remuneration" and to keep employee-indicative clauses out of the agreement.
  • Do not sign with two practices to keep your options open. Guidance Note 3.9.1 says it is neither advisable nor appropriate to enter agreements with more than one practice in order to pick and choose later.
  • Routes that are not a Singapore firm: pupillage with, or reading in the chambers of, a UK barrister or Scottish advocate of more than 7 years' standing; supervised training at a foreign law practice under a formal arrangement (a UK or Australian training contract can do double duty); or work as a Judicial or Legal Service Officer, or under a qualifying relevant legal officer of not less than 5 years' standing. Those thresholds attach to your supervisor, not to you — and "more than 7" excludes a supervisor at exactly 7 years, while "not less than 5" includes one at exactly 5.
  • The public-sector supervisor route covers seven bodies, not the whole civil service. A qualifying relevant legal officer must first be a relevant legal officer, which means a Judicial or Legal Service Officer or a legal officer of a body prescribed by rules. The prescribed list is exactly: IRAS, IPOS, Singapore Land Authority, the Maritime and Port Authority, the National Environment Agency, PUB, and the Public Defender's Office. MAS, CPF Board, LTA, HDB, JTC, EDB and IMDA are not on it, so their in-house lawyers cannot supervise RLT under this limb. Do not spend effort there.
  • The Legal Service Officer route is largely circular for you. The Legal Service Commission requires applicants to already be a qualified person — which for an overseas graduate means Part A plus completed RLT. It therefore sits downstream of RLT rather than being a way to serve it. The exception is the conditional appointment pathway for undergraduates past their second year.
  • Government legal internships do not count. The Legal Service Commission runs a biannual programme across 17 agencies, but these are internships for undergraduates and JD students, and SILE is explicit that intern work is not RLT. Treat them as relationship-building.
  • You may not need RLT at all — there is an alternative limb. Becoming a qualified person requires relevant legal training or relevant legal practice or work: active practice as a legal practitioner in a jurisdiction other than Singapore or as a foreign lawyer in Singapore, or work as legal counsel at an entity whose equity securities are listed on an exchange anywhere. Same six-month duration. If you are already qualified elsewhere, this is often the cleaner route.
  • Recent changes worth knowing: a mandatory Ethics and Professional Responsibility online module applies to Part A candidates from 2026 Session 2; practice training contract registration has been online-only since May 2025; and the public-service training framework applies to trainees starting from 1 January 2026.
