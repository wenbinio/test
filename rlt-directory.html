Rules that decide whether your six months counts

Get these wrong and the period is disregarded. SILE is the only authority.

  • 6 months inside a continuous 8-month window, counted in full or half days. Weekends and public holidays count toward the period but cannot be your start or end date.
  • Finish before Part B begins — all six months must be complete by the last working day before the course starts. Leave must be made up at the end, which pushes your finish date, so leave a buffer.
  • Paralegal or internship work does not count unless formalised as a supervised training arrangement and certified by the practice. Get it in writing at the start, never retroactively.
  • No SILE registration needed beforehand — but you must submit a certification of completion from the training organisation, on SILE's template, at the end. Confirm the firm knows it will sign one.
  • You can stitch periods together across more than one organisation, so long as the total hits six months inside the eight-month window.
  • The 1 July moratorium applies to practice training contracts, not RLT. Lee & Lee says so on its own careers page. If a firm tells you to wait, ask which one they mean.
  • In-house is limited. SILE's guidance indicates the employer must be listed on an official securities exchange — a private limited company generally will not qualify. Confirm your specific employer with SILE in writing before committing.
  • Pay is unregulated. No mandated minimum; Guidance Note 3.9.1 asks only that basic transport and meal costs be covered. Lee & Lee's published $3,000/month is a fair large-firm benchmark. The term of art is honorarium.

If nothing here fits

Three routes that are not a Singapore law firm, and where to look for seats this page misses.

  • Pupillage in England, Wales or Scotland — as a pupil of, or reading in the chambers of, a barrister or Scottish advocate of 7+ years' standing.
  • A foreign law practice — supervised training in foreign law under a formal training arrangement. A UK or Australian training contract can do double duty, but it must be documented and certifiable.
  • Public service — as a Judicial or Legal Service Officer, or under a Qualifying Relevant Legal Officer of 5+ years' standing.
  • Speculative approaches work. A six-month RLT seat is cheaper for a firm to say yes to than a training contract.
