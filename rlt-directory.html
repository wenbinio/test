Before you apply: RLT is not the training contract

The single most common mix-up. These are two separate requirements, served at different times, often applied for through different forms at the same firm.

RLT (Relevant Legal Training) — 6 months. Only overseas Scheduled University graduates need it. It is what makes you a qualified person. It must be finished before the Part B course starts.

PTP / PTC (Practice Training Period / Contract) — 12 months. Everyone needs it, local and overseas. It comes after Part B and leads to admission as an advocate and solicitor.

So an overseas graduate does roughly: RLT → Part A → Part B → 12-month PTP → call to the Bar. Several firms run two separate application listings for these — apply to the right one.

The three routes that aren't a Singapore law firm

SILE recognises four categories of RLT. Most applicants only ever consider the first. If you are struggling for a seat, or you are already training abroad, the other three are worth pricing in.

  • Pupillage in England, Wales or Scotland. Training as a pupil, or reading in the chambers of, a barrister or Scottish advocate of at least 7 years' standing counts as RLT. Useful if you are already on the Bar route in the UK.
  • A foreign law practice. Supervised training in the practice of foreign law, under a formal training arrangement with a foreign law practice, counts. In practice this means a UK, Australian or Malaysian training contract or seat can do double duty — but it must be a formal training arrangement, documented and certifiable, not a summer job.
  • Public service and in-house. Work as a Judicial or Legal Service Officer, or under the supervision of a Qualifying Relevant Legal Officer (an advocate and solicitor of at least 5 years' standing), counts. Watch the in-house limit: SILE's guidance indicates the employer must be listed on an official securities exchange — a private limited company generally does not qualify. Confirm your specific employer with SILE in writing before you commit six months to it.

The cycle, in order

Dates below are the pattern for the currently live cycle. Firms move by a week or two either side.

1 July
The gate opens. The moratorium date. Most firms open practice training contract applications for the intake two years out — 1 July 2026 opened the 2028 PTC round.
Mid-July
RLT-specific listings appear. Drew & Napier opens 2027 RLT on 20 July. Rajah & Tann and Lee & Lee run their RLT listings alongside the TC round.
July – September
Main window. Bird & Bird ATMD closes 30 September. Kennedys opens its process in September. Legal Service Commission undergraduate applications close 7 September.
August
RLT commences for the cohort that applied the previous year — six months, ending before the Part B course.
Rolling
Everything else. Smaller practices, Virtus Law and most Law Society portal listings accept applications year-round and fill as they go. Apply early rather than in season.

How to send one

The pack is close to identical across firms. Build it once, then tailor the cover letter.

  1. One PDF, correctly named. Several firms require the whole application consolidated into a single file — Rajah & Tann caps it at 12MB. Name it Surname_FirstName_RLT2027.pdf.

  2. Cover letter, one page. Name your preferred practice areas and why that firm. Firms that ask for seat preferences generally want no more than two.

  3. CV, up to three pages. Include your predicted classification and current GPA/CAP. Several Singapore firms still expect academic history from secondary school onwards — O-levels, A-levels or equivalent — and Drew & Napier asks for a photograph and nationality.

  4. Transcripts. Latest available, including the term immediately preceding your application. Unofficial is normally fine at application stage.

  5. Say which thing you are applying for. Put "Relevant Legal Training" and the year in the subject line or first sentence. Virtus Law asks for full name, target department and intended Bar admission year in the email subject.

  6. Keep the certification in mind from day one. At the end you must submit a written certification of completion from the training organisation, on SILE's template. Confirm before you accept that the firm knows it will be signing one.

Things worth knowing

  • The moratorium does not bite on RLT. The 1 July gate applies to practice training contracts, not Relevant Legal Training. Lee & Lee states this explicitly on its careers page. If a firm tells you to wait, ask whether they mean the TC or the RLT — several will take RLT applications off-cycle.
  • The moratorium rule itself. If you are eligible to start Part B in July 2027 or later, you may apply for a PTC no earlier than one year before the first day of the month of the earliest Part B session you qualify for. You are exempt if you are already a Qualified Person or an Eligible Person, and grandfathered if you secured a contract before 1 October 2024 for a January 2028 or later start.
  • Paralegal and internship work does not count. Unless it is formalised as a supervised training arrangement and certified as such by the practice. Do not assume a long internship converts retroactively — get it in writing at the start.
  • You can stitch periods together. RLT can be accumulated across more than one organisation, so long as the total reaches six months inside a continuous eight-month window.
  • Weekends and public holidays count toward the period but cannot be your start or end date. Leave is deducted in half-day and full-day units and must be made up at the end, which pushes your completion date — plan a buffer before Part B rather than finishing to the day.
  • Pay varies enormously and is not regulated. The Law Society deliberately declined to mandate a minimum, and Guidance Note 3.9.1 asks only that a practice cover a trainee's basic transport and meal expenses. Lee & Lee publishes $3,000/month for RLT, which is a reasonable benchmark for a large firm. Note the term of art is honorarium, not salary or allowance.
  • An RLT seat is not an offer of a training contract. At most firms they are separate decisions with separate application rounds — though performing well in RLT is the single best route into that firm's TC. Ask directly where the firm stands before accepting.
  • Public service trainees have extra machinery. The framework applying from 1 January 2026 sets a 12-month PTP, requires a mandatory three-month attachment at AGC or a Singapore law practice for those supervised by a QRLO, and requires SILE approval of the manner of service at least 14 days before training starts. Miss that window and the period served may not count.

Where to find seats this page does not list

  • Law Society Careers Portal — practice training listings. The closest thing to a central register. Participation by practices is voluntary, so it is indicative rather than exhaustive, but it is the best single source for smaller firms.
  • Law Society Careers Portal — full job search. Search "relevant legal training" here; some practices post RLT under general vacancies rather than the training-contract section.
  • SILE — Relevant Legal Training and its RLT FAQs. Read both before you accept anything unusual.
  • Law Society — guide to the practice training framework, including honorarium guidance.
  • Firms with no advertised trainee page still take speculative RLT approaches — RHTLaw Asia, Oon & Bazul, Tan Kok Quan Partnership, Wee Swee Teow, PDLegal, Providence Law Asia, Setia Law, Duane Morris & Selvam, Morgan Lewis Stamford, Pinsent Masons MPillay, Ashurst ADTLaw, CMS Holborn Asia, Clyde & Co Clasis, HFW, Watson Farley & Williams and Reed Smith / Resource Law among them. Write to the practice manager or HR address on the firm's contact page; six-month RLT seats are cheaper for a firm to say yes to than a training contract.
